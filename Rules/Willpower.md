 Every character has a reservoir of Willpower that can push them beyond their comfort zone enabling them to endure resistance, enhance their performance, and embrace challenges
# The system
Characters have a pool of will power dice ranging from d4 to d20, they can be spent to do the following:
1. Heal
2. Boost skill checks
3. Perform magic
They can be recovered after a long rest. 
There is one exception to the recovery, burnout. Any time a character undergoes a task they find arduous they exhaust a will power die from their pool. Exhausted dice cannot be recovered after a long rest and instead a character needs to either indulge in **Vices** or recovers between adventures.

--- 
# Healing
During any break roll a will power die to recover that many hp. If combined with a successful medicine check the amount of healing can be increased.
## Examples 
*
+ Perin is a warrior who was injured recently in a skirmish. He is currently at 8/16hp. Sitting down to rest he decides to spend 2d4 willpower dice from his pool and gets a 3 and a 4 for total of 7hp healed. After his rest he is back up to 15/16hp.
+ Elaine is a wizard who was badly injured after experimenting with a magical artifact 3/12hp. The party's cleric Ohm uses material from their medical supplies to heal her. She spends a d6 willpower die and rolls a 2 but she gains an extra +3 from Ohm's successful medicine check and heals +5hp so she is now at 8/12hp*
# Boosting Skill Checks
Before rolling a skill check you can decide to roll willpower dice and add the highest die to the results. 
When rolling a skill check the character has proficiency in they can roll willpower after the result is scored but it cost 2 dice to roll 1 die. 
Willpower dice cannot beat a critical success or failure.
## Examples
+ *Perin is trying to track his companions in woods after a skirmish had them scatter. The skill check is target number 14 and Perin has a skill of 4 at tracking. Perin decides to spend a d4, and d6 and rolls. He get a 10 on his roll in addition to a 4 for his d4 and a 3 for his d6. Adding the highest he gets a total of 14, passing the check.*
+ *Matt is a proficient lockpicker. Currently he is trying to free his party from the cell in Lady Silviana's dungeon before her guards return. The target number is 12 and his skill is 5. Matt then rolls an 11, which would be a failure but because he is proficient he is able to spend 2d4 willpower dice to roll d4 getting a 1 which gets him to the 12 he needs.* 
+ *Elaine is experimenting with a magical artifact to understand its function. Being cautious she spends 2d10 willpower dice wanting to guarantee success. The target number is 14 and her skill is 5. For the 2d10 she gets a high of 10 but she rolls a double 1 on skill check resulting in a critical failure automatically. The artifact explodes dealing her 9 damage.*

# Spell Casting
Spells always cost willpower and will be marked for how much cost. 
Players can substitute lower willpower dice for higher willpower dice.
Wizards can amplify their willpower for spells they can cast by investing their willpower into a focus when preparing.  
## Examples 
+ *Perin has a magic hammer that can cast the spell Smite which costs d6 willpower die. When striking a Trolloc Perin must spend a d6 willpower die if he wants to activate the spell Smite.*
+ *Elaine wants to cast the spell Blinding Light which costs a d4 willpower die, but she is out of d4s. Instead she uses a d6 willpower to cast the spell.*
+ *Elaine is a wizard who uses a jade turtle wand as her focus. The wand can absorb up to 2d4. At the beginning of the day Elaine invests 2d4. The focus then doubles that giving Elaine 4d4 willpower dice that she can use for spells while wielding the wand.*

# Burnout
Exhausted willpower cannot be recovered by long resting.
To recover an exhausted willpower die, the character must take either a protracted rest (between adventures) or indulge their vices.
Willpower dice are exhausted by harrowing experiences, arduous conditions, resisting a vice.
What harrowing or arduous experiences are does depend on the character but below are the standard which should apply unless otherwise stated in the character's sheet. 
Harrowing experiences:
+ Killing another person
+ Torture
+ Near death experiences
Arduous conditions:
+ Sleeping rough
+ Going without or receiving poor quality food or water
+ Physically demanding labour
Indulging in a Vice should always be costly in some way for the character. 
## Examples
+ *Matt needs to plan a break in to rescue his party but has the vice of impulsive. He must exhaust 2d4 willpower dice to resist the urge to leap on in rather than plan.*
+ *Matt before the break in is able to recover a 1d4 die by indulging his Vice of gambling.*
+ *Perin before resting has 2/6 d4, 0/2 d6 willpower dice, he has exhausted 1d4 willpower because he spent the full day marching in the rain. After resting he is at 5/6 d4, 2/2 d6 willpower dice.*  
+ *Perin is able to fully recover any exhausted willpower after he freed his village from Trollocs by resting over the next month.*
+ *Elaine and Nyneave both rest at a cheap inn during their journey. Elaine being of nobility finds these conditions arduous and exhausts a d4 whereas Nyneave coming from a farming village does not.*
+ *Nyneave kills a bandit when she Elaine are ambushed during their journey. Nyneave exhausts a d6 from harrowing experience.*
